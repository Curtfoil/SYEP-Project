Controversy

Nestlé

The term "The Baby Killer" stems from a 1974 report highlighting Nestlé's aggressive marketing of infant formula in developing nations. Promoting formula to impoverished areas without clean water led to widespread infant malnutrition and fatalities from unsafe preparation.

This prompted a libel trial and global boycotts, resulting in the WHO adopting strict international marketing codes for breast-milk substitutes in 1981.

Origins

Henri Nestlé

When Nestlé was first founded in 1866, they were famous for their baby food brands. When Henri Nestlé founded the company, his intentions were to address the high infant mortality rate in Switzerland.

Global Impact

Corporate History & Allegations

Nestlé has faced major controversy on several fronts beyond infant formula. It has faced lawsuits regarding child labor across cocoa supply chains, along with public outrage for pumping groundwater in drought-stricken regions to bottle and sell as natural spring water.
